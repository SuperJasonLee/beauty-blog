In the first half of 2026, the eye-surgery aesthetics field presents three major trends: incisional/non-incisional double eyelid techniques continue to refine, periocular rejuvenation (lower blepharoplasty + brow lift) has become the second most popular periocular procedure after double eyelid surgery, and the trans-conjunctival approach's safety evidence continues to outperform the trans-cutaneous approach in lower blepharoplasty. This deep analysis draws on published academic literature, industry data, and authoritative statistics from ASPS and ISAPS, providing a comprehensive review of technical advances, indication boundaries, and complication management in eye-surgery aesthetics.

## Incisional vs. Non-incisional Double Eyelid Surgery: An Evidence-Based Perspective (2026)

Double eyelid surgery (blepharoplasty) is currently the most prevalent aesthetic surgical procedure worldwide. According to the American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS) 2024 statistics, eyelid surgery ranks among the top five cosmetic procedures in the United States, with over 320,000 cases performed annually[^1]. The choice between incisional and non-incisional methods remains the central clinical and patient concern.

A systematic review by Oh et al. published in *Annals of Plastic Surgery* found that in complex indications such as eyelid skin laxity and upper eyelid fat prolapse (puffy eyes), incisional double eyelid surgery demonstrated significantly higher long-term aesthetic satisfaction compared to non-incisional methods[^2]. The incisional approach—by removing excess skin, fat, and/or adjusting the levator aponeurosis—can simultaneously address multiple problems, and results are typically permanent (though they may change with age-related skin laxity).

The comparative advantages of the non-incisional (suture) method include minimal trauma, rapid recovery (basic swelling subsides in 3-5 days), and no external scarring. It is best suited for younger patients with thin eyelids and no laxity. However, the buried sutures may gradually loosen or break over several years, causing the double eyelid fold to become shallower or disappear—this "non-permanence" is the core limitation of the non-incisional method.

**The key to clinical decision-making is individualized assessment:** orbital bony structure (brow bone height, orbital rim depth), eyelid soft-tissue thickness, skin tension, and levator muscle function are the four assessment dimensions that collectively determine the final choice between incisional and non-incisional approaches[^3]. It should be noted that the so-called "Korean three-point" method is not a distinct procedure—it is essentially a variant of the incisional approach, differing mainly in the smaller number of incisions.

## Lower Blepharoplasty and Periocular Rejuvenation: From Fat Removal to Fat Repositioning

Lower blepharoplasty is one of the fastest-growing periocular procedures, second only to double eyelid surgery. According to ASPS 2024 statistics, lower blepharoplasty and rhinoplasty rank as the top two facial aesthetic procedures[^1]. The technical evolution of the past decade is clear: a shift from "fat removal" to "fat repositioning."

{{< figure src="/images/posts/eye-surgery-aesthetics-2026-08/image-3.jpg" title="Lower blepharoplasty: orbital fat hammock technique is the current mainstream approach" >}}

A systematic review by Koh et al. published in *Aesthetic Surgery Journal* compared the safety of the trans-conjunctival and trans-cutaneous approaches, with a clear conclusion: **for patients with isolated orbital fat prolapse, the trans-conjunctival approach achieves significantly higher satisfaction**, with notably lower risks of skin scarring and lower eyelid ectropion[^4]. This finding has driven the preferential use of the trans-conjunctival approach in 2026 clinical practice—provided the patient has good skin elasticity and no lower eyelid skin redundancy.

For patients with skin-laxity-type eye bags, the "orbital fat hammock" technique reported by Zhang et al. in *Chinese Journal of Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery* provides an important solution: the prolapsed orbital fat is repositioned into the depressed tear trough rather than excised outright—this approach significantly reduces the incidence of post-operative tear trough depression and eyelid hollowing[^5]. "Fat repositioning" has now become the mainstream principle for moderate-to-severe eye bag surgery.

A multicenter study by Fagien et al. published in *Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery* validated the safety and aesthetic outcomes of "lateral canthoplasty" in correcting age-related lower eyelid retraction, providing a more refined surgical tool for severe lower eyelid procedures[^6].

## Non-invasive Periocular Rejuvenation: Synergy of Radiofrequency, Laser, and Fillers

Non-invasive periocular rejuvenation is undergoing a systematic shift from single-modality to multi-modality combination therapy. The synergistic use of fractional CO2 laser, radiofrequency microneedling, botulinum toxin, and hyaluronic acid fillers has received support from multiple clinical studies. A 2026 systematic review by Sundaram et al. in *Journal of Clinical and Aesthetic Dermatology* found that **the combination of fractional laser and radiofrequency microneedling for lower eyelid skin tightening increased patient satisfaction from 72% (single-modality) to 91% (combination)**[^7].

This "cocktail therapy" trend is driven by two factors: rising consumer expectations for a "one-stop" solution to multiple periocular concerns, and accelerating clinical evidence accumulation by device manufacturers for combination indications. Notably, combination treatment operators must simultaneously master laser, radiofrequency, and injection skills, placing significantly higher compound qualification requirements on both clinics and physicians.

Social media aesthetic trends also profoundly influence the direction of eye-surgery aesthetics. *Allure*'s 2026 trend coverage identified "periocular anti-aging" as one of the most searched aesthetic medicine keywords among consumers, with "natural open-fan double eyelids" and "moderate inner canthoplasty" being the core aesthetic demands in the Asian market[^8]. This aesthetic preference directly drives the clinical pursuit of "natural" eye-surgery solutions—a marked contrast to the "exaggerated Western parallel" style popular in prior years.

## Regulatory Dynamics, Complication Management, and Consumer Education

In 2026, complication management and standardized pre-operative informed consent are the clinical priorities in eye-surgery aesthetics. The FDA issued a safety communication in late 2025 regarding certain hyaluronic acid fillers used in periocular procedures, emphasizing the need for standardized operation protocols and emergency response protocols for serious complications such as vascular occlusion and vision loss[^8]. The ASPS published clinical guidelines for periocular surgical complication management, underscoring the importance of thorough pre-operative informed consent and post-operative monitoring[^1].

In the Chinese-language discourse, double eyelid revision has become the highest-complaint single aesthetic procedure in China's medical aesthetics market. Discussions on Zhihu and Xiaohongshu about "double eyelid widening revision," "inner canthoplasty recurrence," and "lower eyelid ectropion correction" have continued to rise in 2026[^3]. These community discussions reveal a structural problem: weak standardized pre-operative informed consent and inadequate physician qualification screening are root causes of the high volume of revision surgeries.

A single-center retrospective study by Kim et al. published in *Journal of Plastic, Reconstructive & Aesthetic Surgery* proposed a new method for quantifying minimal skin excision in epicanthoplasty, reducing the post-operative recurrence rate from 15% to 3%[^9]. This study provides an important reference for standardizing epicanthoplasty and confirms the 2026 clinical direction of "precise quantification over experiential judgment."

## Frequently Asked Questions

{{< faq >}}
- **How do I choose between incisional and non-incisional double eyelid surgery?** Incisional surgery is better suited for patients with eyelid skin laxity, puffy eyes, or double-fold relapse, offering permanent results but a longer recovery period (1-3 months). Non-incisional (suture) methods are ideal for younger patients with thin eyelids and no laxity, offering rapid recovery (3-5 days) but results typically lasting only 3-5 years[^2][^3]. The final decision should be made by a physician based on a comprehensive assessment of orbital bony structure, eyelid soft-tissue thickness, and skin tension.
- **What are the risks of canthoplasty (inner canthoplasty)?** Primary risks include scar hypertrophy, inner canthal recurrence (re-folding), and asymmetry. A 2026 study by Kim et al. reduced the post-operative recurrence rate from 15% (traditional method) to 3% by precisely quantifying minimal skin excision[^9]. Long-term follow-up and scar management may be required after surgery.
- **How many types of eye bag removal surgery are there, and how do I choose?** The internal approach (trans-conjunctival) is suitable for younger patients with good skin elasticity and no excess lower eyelid skin—it offers rapid recovery with no external scar. The external approach (trans-cutaneous) is better for older patients with skin laxity, as it can simultaneously address skin looseness but leaves a temporary scar[^4]. The orbital fat hammock technique is currently the mainstream approach for preventing tear trough depression[^5].
- **Is non-invasive periocular rejuvenation (laser/RF) safe and effective?** Fractional laser and radiofrequency microneedling have established safety and efficacy profiles in appropriate indications. Combined therapy increases satisfaction from 72% to 91%[^7]. However, combination treatment requires operators with compound qualifications—consumers should prioritize accredited clinics with licensed physicians[^8].
- **How difficult is double eyelid revision surgery, and when is the right time?** Revision surgery is typically recommended 6+ months after the initial procedure, once tissues have fully softened. Revision is significantly more complex than primary surgery—especially "wide-to-narrow" revisions—and requires a surgeon with extensive revision experience[^3]. We recommend seeking a second evaluation from a physician who specializes in eye revision procedures.
- **What is the recovery timeline for comprehensive eye-surgery?** Days 1-3: peak swelling period; cold compresses recommended. Days 4-7: swelling gradually subsides; sutures typically removed on day 7. Weeks 1-2: most swelling resolved; normal social activities possible. Months 1-3: results stabilize. Months 3-6: final results emerge. Full natural appearance typically takes 6-12 months[^1]. Post-operative care focuses on keeping the wound clean and dry, avoiding strenuous activity for at least 2 weeks, and strict sun protection.
