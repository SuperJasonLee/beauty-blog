Face slimming (facial contouring) is one of the highest-demand — yet most heavily over-commercialized — segments of aesthetic medicine. In the first half of 2026, peer-reviewed literature and Chinese professional communities are signaling several structural shifts: **masseter botulinum toxin** injection has received systematic evidence-based review, with its morphometric effects and targeted-injection mechanism further clarified; **buccal fat removal** is returning to rational indications after the "influencer face" trend has faded; **mandibular angle contouring (jaw reduction / bone shaving)** continues to advance toward precision and safety; and the boundaries between facial liposuction and non-surgical slimming are being redefined. ## Key Takeaways

- Masseter botulinum toxin ("slimming injections") gained support from multiple systematic reviews and prospective studies in 2026; meta-analysis confirms efficacy for masseter hypertrophy, but refined management of the injection target (motor endplate zone) and injection frequency has become the new clinical focus.
- Buccal fat removal is returning to rational indications after the "influencer face" aesthetic has faded — over-removal causes premature midface hollowing and accelerated aging; 2026 consensus emphasizes "moderation, reversibility first, and caution in young patients."
- Mandibular bony contouring (bone shaving, outer-cortex splitting, T-chinoplasty) continues to become more precise, but as high-risk bony surgery, its 3D preoperative assessment, neurovascular protection, and postoperative management remain the safety core.
- Face slimming is fundamentally a "layered diagnosis": muscle hypertrophy calls for botulinum toxin, fat accumulation for liposuction/buccal fat removal, and bony width for contouring surgery — misattribution is the leading cause of poor results and complications.
- The Chinese market's discussion of "stiff face from slimming injections," "bone-shaving risks," and "buccal fat removal sequelae" keeps intensifying; Zhihu and aesthetic-medicine verticals are the primary channels for pre-op information, though quality varies widely.

## Masseter Botulinum Toxin: From "Slimming Injection" to Evidence-Based Targeting

Masseter botulinum toxin injection (colloquially the "slimming injection") is one of the most intensively discussed face-slimming procedures in the 2026 literature. A systematic review and meta-analysis on masseter muscle hypertrophy[^9] systematically evaluated the efficacy of type A botulinum toxin, confirming reproducible clinical effects in reducing masseter volume and lower-face width. In parallel, a 3-month prospective study[^2] used 3D morphometric measurement to quantify the dynamic post-injection changes of the masseter — providing a more objective basis for the "dose-response curve."

Even more noteworthy is the refinement of the injection target. An anatomical review of "motor endplate zone-targeted injection"[^3] argues that precisely injecting botulinum toxin into the motor-endplate-dense region of the masseter can achieve more durable volume reduction at lower doses, while reducing side effects such as facial stiffness and chewing weakness. This approach is changing the previous empirical model of "even injection by feel."

{{< figure src="/images/posts/face-slimming-2026-07/image-3.jpg" title="The core of masseter botox is targeted injection: precise motor-endplate localization for low dose, lasting effect, fewer side effects" >}}

In clinical practice, patients should be reminded that slimming-injection effects typically appear 2-4 weeks after injection, last 4-6 months, and require repeat injection to maintain. Long-term regular injection can cause masseter atrophy, and improper injection placement or dosage can lead to stiffness, unnatural smiling, chewing weakness, or even localized hollowing[^1]. The extensive Zhihu discussions on "the truth about facial stiffness" and "injection frequency" reflect the information asymmetry that accompanies this procedure's popularization.

## Buccal Fat Removal: From "Influencer Face" to Rational Indications

Buccal fat removal once surged in popularity alongside the "influencer face" / "small V-face" aesthetic. But entering 2026, as beauty standards return to natural, the industry's stance has grown more cautious.

The core controversy centers on **reversibility and long-term aging**. The buccal fat pad (Bichat's fat pad) plays an important role in maintaining midface fullness and supporting soft tissue. Over-removal causes premature midface hollowing, deepened nasolabial folds, and an aged appearance — a process amplified with age as fat naturally depletes. High-traffic Zhihu discussions such as "facial liposuction vs. buccal fat removal" and "who is suitable for buccal fat removal"[^1] reflect strong patient demand for indication guidance.

{{< figure src="/images/posts/face-slimming-2026-07/image-4.jpg" title="Buccal fat removal emphasizes indication control: suitable for younger patients with obvious lower-face fat accumulation and good skin tension" >}}

The 2026 clinical consensus leans toward: buccal fat removal is best suited to those with **obvious lower-face (especially cheek) fat accumulation, an overall rounded face, relatively young age, and good skin elasticity**; whereas those with a naturally thin face, mild pre-existing submalar hollowing, or older age should be especially cautious. "Moderate removal, less is more" is the key principle for reducing long-term risk. It is also essential to distinguish "fatty baby-face fullness" from "muscular/bony lower-face width" — buccal fat removal in the latter is not only ineffective but may worsen facial hollowing and disproportion.

## Mandibular Contouring & Bony Slimming: Precision and Safety Boundaries

For patients with bony lower-face width (mandibular angle hypertrophy, square face), non-surgical means (botox, liposuction) are often limited in effect, and mandibular bony contouring becomes an option. The 2026 literature continues to advance precision in this area. A comparative study[^8] evaluated the differences between "total mandibular inferior border circumferential osteotomy" and "T-chinoplasty combined with mandibular angle contouring," providing evidence-based reference for bony-contouring plan selection across different face shapes.

It must be emphasized that bone shaving, outer-cortex splitting (removing the outer cortical bone), and mandibular angle osteotomy are **high-risk bony surgeries**. Zhihu community posts such as "my personal bone-shaving experience — outer-cortex splitting plus masseter reduction" attract enormous attention[^1], reflecting both strong patient information demand and a warning: the bleeding risk, inferior alveolar nerve injury risk, and long-term post-op swelling and asymmetry risk of such surgery all require an accredited maxillofacial facility, an experienced surgeon, and strict 3D imaging (CBCT) preoperative assessment with neurovascular localization.

{{< figure src="/images/posts/face-slimming-2026-07/image-5.jpg" title="Bony contouring is a high-risk procedure: 3D imaging assessment, neurovascular protection, and accredited facilities are the safety baseline" >}}

Orthognathic surgery (combined orthodontic-orthognathic treatment) also shows related progress in improving facial width and occlusal relationships — a study[^4] quantified inter-arch discrepancies during orthognathic surgery, indicating that bony slimming cannot be viewed in isolation from occlusal function. For patients with both occlusal problems and bony width, a combined orthognathic approach often balances function and aesthetics better than bone shaving alone.

## Layered Diagnosis: The Underlying Logic of Face-Slimming Plan Selection

Synthesizing the procedures above, 2026 clinical and community discussions convey a shared core logic: **face slimming is not a contest between single techniques, but the result of layered diagnosis**. The causes of facial width can be divided into three layers —

- **Muscle layer (masseter hypertrophy)**: suited to masseter botulinum toxin — non-invasive, reversible, but requires repetition;
- **Fat layer (subcutaneous fat, buccal fat accumulation)**: suited to facial liposuction or buccal fat removal, but requires strict indication and volume control;
- **Bone layer (mandibular/zygomatic bony width)**: requires bony contouring surgery — dramatic effect but highest risk.

High-upvote Zhihu discussions such as "my body slimmed down but my face is still big" and "how can big-faced people slim their face effectively"[^1] precisely illustrate that many patients' confusion stems from "misattribution" — mistaking bony width for a fat problem, or fat accumulation for muscle hypertrophy — leading to the wrong plan, wasted money, and no result. Therefore, consulting a qualified physician for a layered in-person assessment is the essential first step in any face-slimming decision. ASPS and ISAPS industry reports likewise consistently emphasize that individualized preoperative diagnosis and realistic expectation management are fundamental to reducing disputes and improving satisfaction.

## Frequently Asked Questions

{{< faq >}}
- **How long do slimming injections (masseter botox) last? Will there be rebound?** Slimming injections typically show effect 2-4 weeks after injection, last about 4-6 months, then the masseter gradually recovers, requiring repeat injection. Long-term regular injection can gradually atrophy the masseter and stabilize the effect. After stopping, the masseter may gradually return to its original volume (i.e., "rebound"), but usually not beyond the pre-treatment level. Note that improper placement or dosage may cause side effects such as stiffness and chewing weakness[^1][^2][^9].
- **Will slimming injections cause a "stiff face"?** "Stiff face" is mostly related to injection placement error (affecting the muscles of expression) or excessive dosage. Proper masseter botox should be precisely localized to the motor-endplate-dense region of the masseter, avoiding effects on expression muscles such as the zygomaticus major[^3]. Choosing an accredited facility, a qualified physician, and genuine toxin significantly reduces this risk.
- **Is buccal fat removal permanent? Will it cause an aged appearance?** Buccal fat removal is usually permanent (removed fat cells do not regenerate). Therefore over-removal is hard to reverse — the midface will hollow prematurely and look aged as fat naturally depletes with age. The 2026 clinical consensus emphasizes "moderate removal, less is more," and it is better suited to young patients with obvious lower-face fat accumulation and good skin elasticity[^1].
- **Is bone shaving / mandibular surgery high-risk? How long is recovery?** Mandibular angle osteotomy, bone shaving, and outer-cortex splitting are high-risk bony surgeries. Main risks include bleeding, inferior alveolar nerve injury (which can cause lower-lip numbness), post-op asymmetry, and prolonged swelling. Post-op swelling usually takes 1-3 months to noticeably subside, with full stabilization at 6-12 months. Always choose an accredited maxillofacial facility, and complete CBCT 3D imaging assessment preoperatively[^1][^8].
- **My face is big — which slimming method should I choose?** The key is "layered diagnosis": if it's masseter hypertrophy (obvious cheek bulge when clenching), slimming injections are suitable; if it's fat accumulation (rounded lower face, pinchable fat), consider facial liposuction or buccal fat removal; if it's bony width (flared mandibular angle, square face), bony contouring surgery is needed. Most people are mixed-type and need a physician's comprehensive in-person judgment — do not self-attribute[^1].
- **Why is it that "my body slimmed down but my face is still big"?** This usually means the facial width is not a simple fat problem — it may be masseter hypertrophy (muscular type) or mandibular bony width (bony type), neither of which can be improved by weight loss. Blindly dieting is not only ineffective but may leave other areas looking gaunt as facial fat depletes. The correct approach is to seek a layered medical assessment[^1].
